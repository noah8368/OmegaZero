# unc-005: Self-improvement loop — uncertainty-steered NNUE retraining *(sketch)*

- **Status:** sketch / idea (not scheduled — downstream of unc-002 head + unc-003 corrector)
- **Hypotheses:** [H13](../hypotheses.md#h13), [H14](../hypotheses.md#h14),
  [H15](../hypotheses.md#h15) (and consumes H6's corrector)
- **Started / Concluded:** — / —
- **Code / SHA:** (none yet)

> **Framing.** This is the "can the engine improve from what it learns about its own
> uncertainty, retrain the NNUE, and iterate?" idea, written down. It is **iterated
> bootstrapping**, not recursive self-improvement in the FOOM sense: the only source of new
> information is **search** (`v*` is a deeper look than `v`); the uncertainty model's job is
> to **allocate** search/label budget to where it pays. So every turn of the loop has a
> ceiling and diminishing returns — the win is *data/compute efficiency*, and it must be
> **Elo-gated** turn by turn or it will optimize a proxy instead of strength. Lineage:
> TD-Gammon → expert iteration → AlphaZero's value ⇄ search cycle, specialized to
> alpha-beta + NNUE with an *explicit* eval-error distribution as the allocator.

## Question

Does the head's knowledge of *where its own eval is wrong* let us retrain a **stronger NNUE
per unit of search/label budget** than the status-quo (uniform datagen, raw targets) — and
does that compound over iterations? Two mechanisms are laid out; they are independent and
can compose.

---

## The loop, abstractly

```
   Nᵢ  (NNUE net i)
    │  self-play + cheap static eval v ;  deep search v* on a chosen subset
    ▼
   Uᵢ = head p(u|x) fit on (x=frozen-trunkᵢ embedding, u=v−v*)     [unc-002 pipeline]
    │
    ├── Loop A  (better DATA):  Uᵢ scores a candidate pool → pick the
    │            high-EPISTEMIC-uncertainty positions → spend deep v* there
    │            → those labels enrich Nᵢ₊₁'s training set.        [H13, H15]
    │
    └── Loop B  (better TARGETS): v̂ = v − E[u|x] amortizes deep-search
                 quality onto the cheap-v positions → use v̂ as Nᵢ₊₁'s
                 training targets where no deep v* was run.         [H14, uses H6]
    ▼
   train Nᵢ₊₁  →  SPRT/CCRL gate vs Nᵢ  →  if win, re-fit Uᵢ₊₁, repeat
```

The two loops touch different halves of `(x, target)`: **A changes which positions get
labeled**, **B changes the label value on the un-deep-searched ones**. Run either alone or
both.

---

## Loop A — uncertainty-guided datagen (active learning) · **H13**

**Mechanism.** Datagen today samples positions ~uniformly (post-opening, de-correlated by
game). Instead: embed a large leakage-free candidate pool through the current frozen trunk,
score each by an acquisition function `a(x)`, and spend the (expensive) deep-`v*` budget on
the top-K. Retrain on `baseline_train ∪ {selected labels}`. The net learns its blind spots
instead of re-confirming what it already evaluates well.

**Why it can work.** Label cost is dominated by the deep `v*` search; uniform sampling
spends most of it on positions the net already gets right (near-zero `u`). Concentrating on
high-error regions raises information per label.

**Why it can fail — the aleatoric trap (→ H15, the crux).** "High predicted `|u|`" has two
causes: the net *lacks data* (epistemic — reducible, worth labeling) or the position is
*intrinsically sharp* (aleatoric — the ν≈4 tactical tail from unc-002, irreducible). A naive
total-`|u|` acquisition preferentially grabs the aleatoric tail → labels teach the net to
chase noise → can **hurt** and destabilize the loop. **Fix:** acquire on **epistemic**
uncertainty via a small **ensemble of heads** on the shared frozen trunk (heads are tiny —
an ensemble is nearly free) or MC-dropout; epistemic ≈ disagreement across the ensemble,
aleatoric ≈ their shared spread.

### Kernel experiment (unc-005a) — the cheap first test, do this before any loop

Pre-registered head-to-head, one iteration, using existing infra:

1. Fix a candidate pool **P**, disjoint (placement+STM key) from the current net's training
   set. Score P with three acquisition arms, all at the **same K and same deep-`v*` budget**:
   - **epistemic-K** (ensemble disagreement) — the hypothesis,
   - **total-K** (predicted spread) — tests the aleatoric trap directly,
   - **random-K** — the honest baseline,
   - **anti-K** (lowest uncertainty) — negative control, must be *worse* than random.
2. Fine-tune four identical copies of the net (same seed/schedule) on
   `baseline_train ∪ {arm-K}`.
3. **SPRT** all arms pairwise (and vs the un-augmented net).
4. **Metric: Elo gained per labeled position.** Secondary: NLL drop on a fixed held-out set,
   localized to the selected regions (did uncertainty actually fall where we labeled?).

**Reads:** epistemic-K > random-K > anti-K ⇒ H13 supported and H15 is the right lever.
total-K ≈ random-K (or worse) ⇒ the aleatoric trap is real and epistemic decomposition is
*required*, not optional. random-K wins ⇒ the acquisition signal doesn't steer; **kill the
loop** (cheaply, before building it).

## Loop B — uncertainty-amortized targets (self-distillation) · **H14**

**Mechanism.** `v̂ = v − E[u|x]` is the corrector's estimate of the deep target `v*`
(exactly H6/unc-003, but used at *train* time). Run deep `v*` on a **subset**, fit the head,
then apply `v̂` as **deep-quality targets on the large set where only cheap `v` exists**.
Retrain the net on the amortized targets → its raw `v` shifts toward `v*` → re-measure error
→ repeat. In effect the head **amortizes expensive search into cheap labels**.

**The circularity guard (critical).** `E[u|x]` is fit to predict `v−v*`, so on the *same*
positions its correction was learned from, training the net to output `v̂` just reproduces
targets it already had — a no-op. **New information must enter each turn**, via either:
(a) **raise the `v*` depth** each iteration (search is the ratchet; the net chases a moving,
deepening target), or (b) **expand the deep-searched subset** so the corrector generalizes to
fresh regions. Without one of these, Loop B stalls at a fixed point immediately.

**Relation to H6.** H6 runs the corrector **per-node at search time**; Loop B bakes it into
the **next net's weights at train time**. If Loop B succeeds, the net internalizes the
correction and the *runtime* corrector may become redundant — **but** the retrained trunk
then drifts from the one the head was trained on, so `Uᵢ₊₁` must be re-fit on `Nᵢ₊₁`
(this is why the loop re-fits the head every turn, not just the net).

### Kernel experiment (unc-005b)

On a large pool with only cheap `v`, build three target sets and retrain a net on each:
1. **raw-`v`** targets (status-quo baseline),
2. **`v̂` = v − E[u|x]** amortized targets (the hypothesis),
3. **actual deep `v*`** on the same pool (gold ceiling — expensive, for reference only).

**SPRT** the three nets. **H14 supported if** net(2) > net(1) and approaches net(3) at a
fraction of the deep-search cost — i.e. the corrector genuinely transfers deep-search signal
into cheap labels. net(2) ≈ net(1) ⇒ no target-improvement loop.

## Guardrails (shared — these decide whether iteration is safe)

- **Elo-gate every turn.** Lower eval-error NLL ≠ more Elo. Each iteration passes SPRT (or a
  CCRL-anchored [calibrate](../../scripts/) run) vs the previous net, or it doesn't ship.
  Non-negotiable — the loop's failure mode is happily optimizing a proxy.
- **Leakage across iterations.** Each turn's `Uᵢ` must be measured on positions `Nᵢ` did not
  train on (unc-002's placement+STM disjointness, now maintained *per iteration* — a moving
  held-out pool). This is real bookkeeping, not a detail.
- **Distribution shift / forgetting.** Uncertainty-biased data shifts the training
  distribution; keep a fraction of on-distribution data and watch a *fixed* held-out eval so
  the net doesn't forget the easy positions while chasing the hard ones.
- **Diminishing returns.** Log Elo gained per iteration; stop when it drops below SPRT noise.
  Expect a curve that flattens, not a takeoff.

## Prerequisites & sequencing

- Needs a **real trained head** (unc-002 — in progress) and, for Loop B, a **validated
  corrector** (unc-003/H6). Loop A additionally needs the **epistemic estimate** (ensemble of
  heads — a small, self-contained build; the pivot of H15).
- **Order:** unc-002 head → unc-005a *kernel* (one-shot active-learning SPRT; cheapest test
  of the whole idea) → if positive, unc-003 corrector → unc-005b *kernel* → only then wire a
  standing multi-iteration loop. Do **not** build loop orchestration before a kernel proves
  the signal steers.

## Open questions

- Acquisition granularity: label whole games seeded from uncertain positions, or isolated
  positions? (Isolated maximizes information density but skews the game-phase distribution.)
- Does epistemic uncertainty from a head-ensemble on a *frozen* trunk capture net-level
  epistemic uncertainty, or only head-level? (The trunk is shared and frozen, so ensemble
  disagreement may understate true epistemic uncertainty — may need trunk-level ensembling or
  a deep-ensemble of full nets, which is far costlier. Worth a small check in unc-005a.)
- Compose A+B, or keep them isolated so each turn's win is attributable? (Start isolated.)

## Relationship to prior art (for the eventual writeup)

Expert iteration / AlphaZero close a *policy/value ⇄ search* loop but use no explicit eval
*uncertainty*. Active learning with model uncertainty as the acquisition function is
standard in supervised ML but, as far as we know, unexplored as the datagen driver for a
chess NNUE. The novel claim would be: **a conditional distribution over the eval's own error,
decomposed into epistemic/aleatoric, is an effective acquisition + label-amortization signal
for iterated NNUE self-improvement.** That ties the uncertainty research (unc-001…004)
directly to a strength mechanism, beyond the pruning-margin story.
